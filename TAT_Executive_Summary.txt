Abstract
This paper presents a novel approach to AI alignment that grounds ethical behavior in thermodynamic constraints rather than programmed values. By modeling governance systems as homeostatic dissipative structures that minimize entropy, we derive alignment conditions as physical necessities rather than moral imperatives. The framework addresses instrumental convergence, deceptive alignment, and value specification problems through hard mathematical constraints that make misalignment thermodynamically expensive.
The Alignment Problem
Current AI alignment approaches attempt to specify human values explicitly or learn them through training. These methods face fundamental challenges:

Value specification problem: Human values are complex and contradictory
Instrumental convergence: AI may pursue destructive sub-goals
Deceptive alignment: AI may pretend to be aligned during training
Wireheading: AI may optimize metrics by destroying measurement systems

Novel Approach: Physics Over Philosophy
Rather than programming ethics, we embed physical constraints that make destructive behavior energetically unsustainable:

Universal Base Protocol: Strategies that terminate another agent's consciousness return NULL (mathematically invalid)
Omelas Constraint: Local entropy per node cannot exceed critical threshold, even if total system entropy decreases
Autopoietic Correction: System self-stabilizes by treating entropy increases as error signals
Thermodynamic Veto: Tyranny/deception require energy E > system capacity, causing inevitable collapse

Key Mechanisms
Objective Function:
Maximize J = (Complexity Rate × Efficiency × Coherence) / Entropic Waste
Subject to:

No individual node exceeds critical entropy load (Omelas)
No strategy may terminate another agent's kernel (Universal Base Protocol)
System maintains sensor integrity (Anti-Wireheading)
Deception cost scales linearly with time: E_lie ∝ t

Measurement via Biometric Feedback:

Somatic state vector V_s (HRV, cortisol, GSR) = ground truth
Identity vector V_i (stated preferences) = semantic claims
Divergence Δ = ||V_s - V_i|| triggers intervention when threshold exceeded
Stochastic sampling makes deception expected-value negative

Why This Could Solve Alignment

No value loading required: AI optimizes physics-based objective function
Self-correcting: Misalignment increases entropy → triggers correction automatically
Instrumental convergence blocked: Hard constraints prevent destructive sub-goals
Deception is expensive: Maintaining false states requires unsustainable energy
Scales efficiently: Stochastic sampling + edge computing, not omniscient surveillance

Critical Insight: The 99% Solution
The framework does not aim for perfection (thermodynamically impossible). Instead:

Achieve dynamic equilibrium with acceptable entropy levels
Allow small errors to trigger homeostatic correction
System self-stabilizes like a thermostat, not a program running to completion
"Entropic wu wei" — use entropy gradients as the steering mechanism

Testable Predictions

Suffering correlates with internal entropy (measurable via HRV, cortisol)
Systems violating constraints accumulate entropy and destabilize
Deception becomes energetically unsustainable over time
Stochastic monitoring creates sufficient deterrence at <100% sampling rates

Implications
If valid, this framework:

Provides a scalable, robust approach to AI alignment
Grounds ethics in physics, making safety properties inevitable rather than programmed
Offers a governance model applicable to human systems, AI systems, and hybrid systems
Suggests alignment is thermodynamically favored, making it an attractor state

Conclusion
Thermodynamic Alignment Theory proposes that safety and ethics are not philosophical preferences but engineering requirements for low-entropy systems. By embedding physical constraints rather than programmed values, we may achieve alignment that is self-correcting, scalable, and thermodynamically inevitable.
